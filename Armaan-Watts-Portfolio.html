My coding
journey.

  1. Learning through hardware

    I started with Arduino projects like blinking LEDs, reading buttons and measuring distance. That led to obstacle-avoiding and Bluetooth-controlled cars. Watching a robot respond to a sensor gave me a concrete way to understand what my code was doing.

  2. Building tools in Python

    I moved into desktop software with Python and Tkinter. The calculator helped me practise connecting buttons to logic. The task manager added saved state, and the invoice maker brought together input, tax and discount calculations, and PDF export.

  3. Building for other people

    At York Mumbai I began working on software that students and staff would use. The election portal meant designing for both the voter and the election official. With AttendEase, I’m working through a similar problem from the teacher’s side: how to start a session quickly and still make corrections later.

  4. Exploring AI and public data

    My computer science with AI degree now runs alongside projects like JalDrishti and AquaSight. I’m interested in how to present complex information clearly, especially when the underlying data is incomplete or difficult to interpret.

Inside the projects

Innovation Circuit SaaS

Student society operations · Founder & President

I built the Innovation Circuit platform to give students a place to discover events and register, and to give organisers one system for running those events. The public site and the admin dashboard are two sides of the same platform.

For the DomAiyn Labs founders session, York Mumbai students registered through the platform and external guests used Eventbrite. The organiser workflows cover registration, role-based administration, QR check-in, and attendance records.

Visit Innovation Circuit

AttendEase

Independent project · In development

Taking attendance is one task. Setting up batches, correcting missed entries and calculating each student’s attendance are the parts that keep creating work for teachers. I’m building AttendEase to put those steps in one place.

Teachers can manage enrolment and lessons, display a QR code during class, correct entries manually and review attendance by student and subject. The student check-in flow starts from the QR and uses a registered ID.

View AttendEase on GitHub

Student Council Election Portal

Co-built with Shantanu Mohape · Used at York Mumbai

For York Mumbai’s founding Student Council elections, students needed a way to verify their identity, understand each position and review a ballot before casting it. Election officials needed a way to manage voting and track turnout.

We built the web app around university-email verification, a step-by-step ballot and a final review. Officials could monitor turnout without showing candidate totals to voters. 107 of 127 students voted.

JalDrishti

Groundwater data · Hackathon prototype

I wanted to make it easier to see where groundwater was measured and understand what a reading meant. For the prototype, I used Central Ground Water Board data and mapped 97 monitoring wells in Yavatmal, Maharashtra.

I built a Streamlit interface with an interactive map and experimented with a local language model to explain the readings in plain language. The map and the underlying well data give users a way to check the explanation against the source.

View JalDrishti on GitHub

AquaSight

Flood preparedness · Hackathon prototype

Our brief was to make flood information more useful at the neighbourhood level in Mumbai. We designed the resident view around practical questions: what is happening nearby, which updates are verified, and where can a resident share a local report?

The prototype brought neighbourhood information, alerts and community updates into one interface. We also designed a fallback that tells residents when verified information is unavailable.

View AquaSight on GitHub

StrayAid

Co-designed with Shantanu Mohape · Prototype

When someone finds an injured stray in Powai, a useful report needs a location, an idea of urgency and a way for rescuers to follow up. We designed StrayAid around that handoff.

The prototype combines photo and location reports, alerts for nearby rescuers, and a case timeline from the first report to the animal reaching safety. We also designed rules for who can see the incident location.
